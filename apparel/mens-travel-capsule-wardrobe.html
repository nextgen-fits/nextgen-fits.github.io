Start with the itinerary and the weather

Start with the itinerary. A city trip with dinners needs different clothes from a warm beach week or a work conference. Check forecast ranges, rain, walking distance and dress codes, then identify which items must perform. Packing a generic list without those constraints creates extra weight.

Make every top work with more than one bottom

Pick a base palette using clothes you already combine easily: for example, navy, charcoal, olive, cream or denim. One accent color adds variety without introducing pieces that only match one outfit. Neutral does not mean every item must be black; the palette should suit your complexion and preferences.

Pack layers that dry and store sensibly

A sample seven-day core might be four tops, two bottoms, an optional short, underwear and socks for each day, a light outer layer and two pairs of shoes. Reduce or add pieces based on laundry, activity and baggage. This is a planning template, not a universal packing rule.

Shoes set the practical limit

Choose fabrics by the trip. Cotton can feel familiar but may take longer to dry; technical blends may pack light but look too sporty for some occasions. Linen breathes but wrinkles. Check garment care and comfort rather than relying on a fabric’s marketing category.

Plan laundry before adding more clothes

Shoes take space, so choose pairs that cover distinct needs: comfortable walking and a dressier or activity-specific option. Wear the bulkier pair during transit. Confirm that both work with the trousers you plan to bring.

Wear the complete outfits before you leave

A single layer should suit the expected temperature range, but rain and cold may require specialized outerwear. Layering a tee, overshirt and shell can provide flexibility where a thick jacket is too warm. Do not trade necessary weather protection for a tidy packing photo.

Leave room for what the trip really needs

Before leaving, build several complete outfits on the bed and photograph them. If a piece appears in only one combination, ask whether it earns its space. Pack one spare top if spills are likely, and leave room for souvenirs or laundry.

Frequently asked questions

How many outfits for a week?

Plan enough combinations for the itinerary and laundry access; repeating pieces is the point of a capsule.

What colors work best?

Choose a small base palette you already wear, then add one accent; personal style and climate matter.

Can one jacket work for every trip?

Only if its warmth and weather protection fit the destination. Layering works better than a universal garment.
