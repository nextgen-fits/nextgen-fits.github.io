Best G-SHOCK Square for Everyday Wear: DW-5600BB or GW-M5610U?

The square has lasted because it knows its job. Choosing your version is less about collecting features and more about reading the time.

Quick Verdict: DW-5600BB vs. GW-M5610U

Buy the GW-M5610U-1 ($100–$135) if you want one G-Shock square to wear every day without thinking about it. For roughly $25–$35 more than the DW-5600BB, you get Tough Solar charging, Multi-Band 6 atomic timekeeping, a crisp positive LCD, and Casio's updated Module 3495 with a Super Illuminator LED, five alarms, World Time, and button-beep mute. Buy the DW-5600BB-1 or updated DW-5600UBB-1 ($75–$100) only if the blackout stealth look is non-negotiable, you live outside Multi-Band 6 radio range, or you prefer a stripped-back beater with zero solar habits—just expect dimmer low-light legibility from the negative display.

On paper, the Casio G-Shock DW-5600BB-1 and GW-M5610U-1 look like siblings separated by a color palette. Both carry the classic 1983 square silhouette, both shrug off 200 meters of water and hard impacts, and both weigh right around 52 to 53 grams on a resin strap. On your wrist every day, though, they feel like two completely different watches—and the $25 to $35 price gap between them buys one of the biggest functional upgrades in Casio's entire lineup.

Price Reality: What $75–$100 vs. $100–$135 Actually Buys You

In real-world retail, the standard DW-5600BB-1 and its LED-updated twin, the DW-5600UBB-1, typically sell between $75 and $100. The GW-M5610U-1 usually sits between $100 and $135. That small spread is why most buyers hesitate—and why most long-term square owners end up wishing they had spent the extra thirty bucks up front.

With the DW-5600BB, you are paying for the stealth, murdered-out aesthetic: matte black resin, zero red or white dial text, and a dark negative LCD. Under the hood, it runs a basic quartz module powered by a single CR2016 coin cell. With the GW-M5610U, you step into Casio's Tough Solar and Multi-Band 6 ecosystem, plus the modernized Module 3495. It is not just a solar version of the 5600; it is a smaller, smarter, more legible daily tool that rarely needs you to open the caseback or touch a setting button.

Negative Display Legibility vs. 5610U Solar Viewing Angles

The single biggest make-or-break factor between these two watches is not solar power or atomic sync—it is whether you can actually read the time at a glance. The DW-5600BB uses a negative LCD (light digits on a dark background). In bright outdoor sunlight or directly under an overhead desk lamp, it looks crisp and unmistakably cool. The moment you step into a dim hallway, a shaded car cabin, or an overcast afternoon, contrast drops fast. You will find yourself tilting your wrist to catch a light reflection or tapping the backlight button far more often than you expect.

By contrast, the GW-M5610U uses a traditional positive LCD (dark digits on a light gray-green background), which remains effortlessly readable across almost any indoor lighting. There is one subtle quirk worth knowing, though: because the GW-M5610U packs a solar panel around the dial perimeter, the LCD sits slightly deeper behind the mineral crystal than on a non-solar 5600. At extreme off-axis angles—say, looking sharply down your forearm from the bottom—you can notice slight LCD ghosting where unlit segments faintly show. In normal straight-on or slightly angled wrist glances, the positive 5610U still beats the negative 5600BB by a wide margin.

If you love the stealth look of the DW-5600BB, the backlight matters. Older DW-5600BB-1 stock runs Module 3229 with Casio's classic blue-green electroluminescent (EL) backlight, which lights the whole screen evenly. Newer DW-5600UBB-1 units run Module 3525, which swaps EL for a brighter, longer-lasting white LED. Neither has an auto-light tilt sensor, whereas the GW-M5610U's Super Illuminator LED lets you toggle 1.5-second or 3-second illumination plus automatic wrist-tilt activation in the dark.

Wrist Fit: 6.25-Inch to 7.5-Inch+ Wrists and Strap Drape

Numbers on a spec sheet can fool you here. The DW-5600BB measures 48.9 × 42.8 × 13.4 mm, while the GW-M5610U measures 46.7 × 43.2 × 12.7 mm. Notice that the GW-M5610U is 2.2 mm shorter lug-to-lug and 0.7 mm thinner. On paper that sounds minor; on a wrist, it changes how the watch drapes.

On smaller wrists (6.25 to 6.75 inches), the GW-M5610U wears noticeably cleaner. The shorter lug-to-lug footprint prevents the resin strap lugs from overhanging the edges of a narrower wrist bone, and the 12.7 mm thickness slides under a flannel or jacket cuff without catching. The DW-5600BB feels slightly blockier and sits a touch prouder off the wrist.

On medium to larger wrists (7.0 to 7.5+ inches), both squares wear effortlessly and weigh practically nothing at 52 to 53 grams. Larger wrists actually give the DW-5600BB's slightly longer 48.9 mm profile room to breathe, making it look balanced rather than oversized. Out of the box, both watches ship on standard Casio matte resin straps—the 5600BB's strap feels slightly stiffer for the first week, while the 5610U's strap curves inward a bit sooner right at the lugs. After two weeks of daily wear and warm showers, both break in comfortably.

Module 3229 / 3525 vs. Module 3495: Daily Usability Differences

This is where the GW-M5610U pulls away for anyone who actually uses their watch beyond telling the time. Both the DW-5600BB (Module 3229) and DW-5600UBB (Module 3525) give you the essentials: time, date, a 24-hour stopwatch, a 24-hour countdown timer with auto-repeat, and a single multi-function alarm with hourly chime and flash alert. There is no World Time, no button-beep mute, and no simultaneous current-time display inside the timer or stopwatch on Module 3229.

The GW-M5610U's Module 3495—the major upgrade over the older 3159—fixes almost every daily annoyance of classic G-Shock squares:

  • Button-Beep Mute: You can silence the mode-switching chirps while keeping your alarms audible.
  • 5 Daily Alarms (1 Snooze): Separate wake-up, medication, or parking-meter alarms instead of juggling one slot.
  • 48-City World Time (5 Quick-Swap Presets): Ideal for travelers or remote workers tracking a second time zone, plus one-button home/world time swap.
  • MM.DD or DD.MM Date Format & Multi-Language Day: Switchable date order so European and US wearers both get their preferred layout.
  • Current Time Visible in Timer & Stopwatch: You can see the time of day while timing a workout or commute.
  • Super Illuminator LED with Afterglow: Choose 1.5s or 3s fade-out instead of an abrupt shutoff.

Tough Solar and Multi-Band 6 in Real-World Ownership

A standard CR2016 battery in the DW-5600BB lasts roughly 2 to 5 years depending on how often you trigger the backlight and flash alert. Replacing it takes five minutes with a small Phillips screwdriver, though you should always check the rubber caseback gasket if you swim with it regularly.

The GW-M5610U replaces routine battery swaps with Tough Solar charging a CTL1616 rechargeable cell. Under normal daily wear—even mostly indoors—the battery indicator stays at "H" (High). If you leave it in a dark drawer for weeks, Power Saving mode blanks the display overnight and wakes it up instantly when light hits the dial. Avoid leaving it on a blazing hot car dashboard to charge; ordinary indirect daylight on a windowsill is plenty.

Multi-Band 6 automatically syncs with six atomic radio towers overnight: Fort Collins (WWVB) in North America, Anthorn (MSF) in the UK, Mainflingen (DCF77) in Germany, Shangqiu (BPC) in China, and Fukushima/Kyushu (JJY) in Japan. If you live inside those footprints and leave the watch near a window overnight—or even on your wrist—you get dead-accurate atomic time every morning without touching a button.

Common Pitfalls & Buying Mistakes to Avoid

Before you click buy, watch out for four common traps that trip up first-time G-Shock square shoppers:

1. US GW-M5610U-1 vs. JDM GW-M5610U-1JF: Both are the exact same watch with the exact same Module 3495, dimensions, and Tough Solar / Multi-Band 6 hardware. The "-1JF" suffix simply indicates Japanese Domestic Market packaging (often a nicer box) and occasionally a higher import markup. Buy whichever is cheaper from a reputable seller.

2. Buying Multi-Band 6 Outside Radio Coverage: If you live in Australia, South America, Southeast Asia, or deep inside a steel-and-concrete apartment far from the six towers, Multi-Band 6 will not auto-sync reliably. The GW-M5610U still runs at standard ±15 seconds/month quartz accuracy, and Tough Solar + Module 3495 still make it worth buying—just do not pay extra expecting atomic sync in Sydney or São Paulo.

3. The Recessed Adjust Button: On both the DW-5600BB and GW-M5610U, the top-left Adjust button sits deeply recessed inside the bezel to prevent accidental presses. On the DW-5600BB in particular, that button is notoriously stiff and requires a thumbnail or pen tip. The GW-M5610U's Adjust button is slightly easier to press, and because Multi-Band 6 handles time and DST automatically, you barely ever need to touch it.

4. Confusing DW-5600BB-1 (EL) and DW-5600UBB-1 (LED): Retailers frequently mix product photos. If you specifically want the nostalgic blue-green EL glow, check the caseback or listing for Module 3229 (DW-5600BB-1). If you want the brighter white LED and longer battery efficiency, look for Module 3525 (DW-5600UBB-1).

Buyer FAQ

Common Questions Before You Buy

Is the G-Shock DW-5600BB solar powered?

No. Both the classic DW-5600BB-1 (Module 3229) and the updated DW-5600UBB-1 (Module 3525) run on a standard non-rechargeable CR2016 lithium coin battery rated for roughly 2 to 5 years depending on backlight and alarm usage. If you want Tough Solar charging with a CTL1616 rechargeable cell, you need the GW-M5610U-1.

Is the DW-5600BB negative display hard to read in daily use?

In bright outdoor sun or directly under a desk lamp, the DW-5600BB negative LCD looks sharp. In dim hallways, shaded cars, or overcast rooms, contrast drops noticeably and you will rely on the backlight or wrist tilting much more often than with the GW-M5610U's positive gray LCD. If one-glance indoor legibility matters more than all-black stealth, choose the GW-M5610U.

What is the difference between Module 3229, 3525, and 3495?

Module 3229 (original DW-5600BB-1) uses a blue-green EL backlight, 1 alarm, no World Time, and no button-beep mute. Module 3525 (updated DW-5600UBB-1) keeps that same basic feature set but switches to a brighter white LED backlight. Module 3495 (GW-M5610U-1) is a major upgrade: Tough Solar, Multi-Band 6 atomic sync, Super Illuminator LED with 1.5s/3s afterglow and Auto-Light, 5 daily alarms, 48-city World Time, DD.MM date format, and button-beep mute.

Will Multi-Band 6 always synchronize everywhere?

No. Multi-Band 6 receives time calibration signals from six atomic towers across North America (WWVB), the UK (MSF), Germany (DCF77), China (BPC), and Japan (JJY). If you live outside those coverage zones—or deep inside a steel-and-concrete building—auto-sync may fail. Placing the watch near a window overnight usually restores reception, and without a signal the watch still runs at ±15 seconds per month quartz accuracy.

What is the difference between GW-M5610U-1 and GW-M5610U-1JF?

None mechanically. Both are the exact same watch with Module 3495, Tough Solar, Multi-Band 6, and identical 46.7 × 43.2 × 12.7 mm dimensions. The "-1JF" suffix only denotes Japanese Domestic Market packaging and often carries an import price premium.

How do they fit on 6.25-inch to 7.5-inch+ wrists?

Despite looking similar, the GW-M5610U (46.7 × 43.2 × 12.7 mm, 52 g) is 2.2 mm shorter lug-to-lug and 0.7 mm thinner than the DW-5600BB (48.9 × 42.8 × 13.4 mm, ~53 g). On smaller 6.25" to 6.75" wrists, the 5610U drapes cleaner with less lug overhang and slides easier under shirt cuffs. On 7.0" to 7.5"+ wrists, both wear comfortably and light.

Why is the top-left Adjust button recessed on both models?

Casio recesses the top-left Adjust button inside the bezel on classic 5600/5610 squares to prevent accidental time-setting changes during impacts or sports. It feels stiffer on the DW-5600BB and requires a fingernail, whereas on the GW-M5610U you rarely need to touch it because Multi-Band 6 handles time and daylight saving updates automatically.

Which one is better value: $75–$100 vs. $100–$135?

At $100–$135, the GW-M5610U-1 is the far stronger long-term value for most buyers—spending ~$25–$35 more eliminates battery swaps, adds atomic accuracy, and dramatically improves indoor display legibility. Only buy the DW-5600BB-1 / DW-5600UBB-1 ($75–$100) if you specifically want the blackout aesthetic.
