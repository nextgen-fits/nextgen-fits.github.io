In this review, we're taking a closer look at the design, movement, wearing experience, and whether the Tissot PRX is really worth your money.

What Makes the PRX Special?

The Tissot PRX is one of the watches that changed the entry-level Swiss watch market. It offers a luxury-inspired integrated bracelet design, a Swiss automatic movement, and impressive specifications at a relatively affordable price.

Design: The Integrated Bracelet

The first thing you notice about the PRX is the integrated bracelet. This style became famous in luxury watches like the Audemars Piguet Royal Oak, but Tissot brought a similar design language to a much more accessible price point. The case has sharp lines, polished edges, and a slim profile that gives the watch a very premium appearance.

The waffle-style dial texture adds depth and catches light beautifully. Available colors include blue, black, green, and silver, making it easy to match different styles. The 40mm case size is versatile for most wrists. It works well with business outfits, casual clothing, and everyday wear.

From a design perspective, this watch looks much more expensive than it actually is.

Movement: The Powermatic 80

Inside the automatic version is the Powermatic 80 movement. The biggest advantage is the impressive 80-hour power reserve. That means if you take the watch off for a few days, it can continue running without needing to reset the time. For an entry-level Swiss automatic watch, this is a strong feature.

The PRX also comes with sapphire crystal, which provides excellent scratch resistance — a meaningful upgrade over mineral glass common at this level. The water resistance is 100 meters, making it suitable for daily activities like swimming and showering.

Wearing Experience

The integrated bracelet gives the PRX a unique feeling on the wrist. It sits close to the case and creates a very smooth appearance. However, the bracelet design does not fit every wrist perfectly. People with smaller wrists may find the 40mm version slightly large. If you have a smaller wrist, the 35mm version may be a better choice.

The Casio Edifice EFR-S108D shares the same integrated bracelet design that makes the PRX so distinctive. At just 7.8mm thick, it's actually slimmer than the PRX, making it even more comfortable on the wrist. The octagonal flat bezel gives it a sporty yet luxurious look that echoes the PRX's Genta-inspired design.

Final Verdict

Should you buy the Tissot PRX? If you want your first Swiss automatic watch, a stylish everyday watch, or something that looks more expensive than it costs, the PRX is one of the strongest choices in this category.

Should you buy the Casio Edifice EFR-S108D? If you love the PRX look but want to save money, or if you prefer the grab-and-go convenience of quartz, the EFR-S108D is an excellent choice.

Overall, the Tissot PRX is not perfect. But it is one of the watches that proves you don't need to spend thousands of dollars to enjoy great watch design.
