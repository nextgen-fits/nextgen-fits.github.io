What to compare first

Start by measuring your wrist circumference and, more usefully, the flat width across the top. Compare that width with the watch’s lug-to-lug measurement. If the lugs extend past the wrist edges, the case can look oversized even when the diameter seems moderate.

How this affects everyday use

Case diameter excludes the crown and does not tell the whole fit story. A round case with short downward-curving lugs may wear compactly; a wider bezel or straight lugs can look larger. Thickness affects how the watch sits under a cuff and whether it rocks during daily movement.

A low-risk way to decide

A mechanical watch is not a precision instrument in the same sense as a phone or quartz watch. Daily rate depends on caliber, regulation, wear position and temperature. If exact timekeeping is your top priority, compare quartz alternatives as well.

Do not overvalue a single headline number. A specification helps narrow options, but the complete experience also depends on materials, design, compatibility, manufacturing variation and maintenance. Frequently asked questions

What size watch suits a small wrist?

There is no universal cutoff. Compare lug-to-lug, case shape and how the lugs sit on your wrist.

Are automatic watches more accurate than quartz?

Typically not; accuracy varies by movement and use. Choose an automatic for its mechanical character, not presumed precision.
