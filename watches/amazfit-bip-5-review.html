The verdict: a large, simple watch that needs a good reason to be the older option

When shopping for an affordable everyday fitness smartwatch, finding a model that balances screen legibility, built-in satellite positioning, and straightforward phone notifications without breaking the bank is tough. The Amazfit Bip 5 makes the most sense when its current retail discount is meaningfully cheaper than newer alternatives, and your day-to-day requirements are straightforward: reading message alerts, tracking casual walks or runs, and taking the occasional hands-free Bluetooth call while your phone sits nearby on a desk.

Its primary appeal is instantly obvious: you get a massive 1.91-inch rectangular screen estate that makes scanning data fields effortless, without having to commit to a premium smartwatch price tier. However, the compromises are just as real and deserve upfront scrutiny. This is an older TFT-screen design running Zepp OS, meaning visual contrast, viewing angles, and power efficiency differ substantially from modern AMOLED panels. Before treating a Bip 5 discount as an unmissable bargain, weigh its actual daily compromises against your personal routine.

If you find that the Bip 5 and a newer AMOLED rival cost nearly the same, choosing the older model becomes hard to justify. Upgraded display technology, improved battery management, and stricter water-resistance specifications on newer watches are substantial daily factors, not just minor cosmetic tweaks. But if the price gap is wide, casual users who primarily want clear notification pop-ups and independent outdoor route tracking will find the Bip 5 to be an extremely pragmatic starting point. Always ask yourself what features your routine genuinely requires before letting a low price tag make the final decision.

The screen: size helps, but TFT and AMOLED are different purchases

Amazfit specifies a generous 1.91-inch TFT touchscreen for the Bip 5. That expansive physical panel gives the software plenty of room to layout notifications, caller IDs, and fitness metrics with large, legible typography. For anyone transitioning from a narrow, cramped fitness band, the sheer scale of the text is a breath of fresh air. You no longer have to squint to read an incoming email preview or your active pace.

However, screen size and screen technology are entirely separate attributes. The TFT panel used here lacks the deep blacks, infinite contrast ratios, and punchy saturation of AMOLED displays. In direct, blazing midday sunlight, a TFT screen relies entirely on backlight power rather than self-illuminating pixels, which can wash out colors and make glanceability trickier unless brightness is manually boosted. If you are accustomed to flagship Apple Watches or recent Amazfit GTR/GTS devices, the Bip 5 display will feel decidedly entry-level.

Ultimately, selecting this screen comes down to your personal tolerance for display quality versus cost. If you primarily look at your watch indoors or during shaded morning jogs, the Bip 5's large TFT matrix gets the job done cleanly. But if vibrant colors, deep blacks, and always-on display (AOD) efficiency are core priorities, the screen technology alone is a compelling reason to step up to an AMOLED alternative.

Case and strap: a light watch can still be a large watch

Reviewing the physical specifications, the Bip 5 measures roughly 45.94 by 38.09 by 11.2 millimeters (excluding the optical sensor bump), while tipping the scales at an ultra-light 26 grams (excluding the silicone strap). On paper, 26 grams sounds featherlight—and on the wrist, it practically vanishes during sleep and typing. Yet, low weight does not automatically mean a small footprint.

Because of its broad rectangular proportions, the watch face spans quite a wide area across the wrist. If you have slender wrists (under 16 centimeters in circumference), the plastic case edges may overhang slightly or catch persistently on long-sleeve shirt cuffs and jacket buttons. Before committing, test it with your typical wardrobe. Ensure the strap allows a snug, stable fit without requiring you to crank it down so tightly that the optical heart-rate sensor digs painfully into your skin.

One major practical advantage of the Bip 5's standard quick-release strap mechanism is aftermarket versatility. If you dislike the stock silicone band—which can trap sweat during intense afternoon workouts—swapping it out for a breathable nylon hook-and-loop strap takes seconds and instantly improves long-term wearing comfort, especially for continuous overnight biometric tracking.

GPS: useful for recording a route, not proof of measured accuracy

One of the strongest arguments for choosing the Bip 5 over basic fitness bands is its built-in satellite positioning, supporting four major constellations (GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, QZSS). This hardware addition means you can leave your smartphone at home when heading out for a morning run or evening bike ride, while the watch still independently logs your exact geographic route and distance.

In everyday use under clear skies, the Bip 5 locks onto satellite signals relatively quickly and paints a recognizable path in the Zepp smartphone companion app. However, built-in standalone GPS in budget wearables always involves compromises. In dense urban environments surrounded by tall concrete high-rises or under heavy tree cover, you may notice minor track drift, where the mapped line wanders slightly across streets or through buildings.

If your goal is casual progress tracking—such as logging your 5K distance or seeing how far your weekend neighborhood walk was—the Bip 5's GPS performs admirably. But if you are a data-obsessed runner training for a marathon where split-second pace accuracy and exact elevation profiles are paramount, dedicated sports watches from specialized running brands remain the gold standard.

Bluetooth calls: keep the phone connection in the picture

The Bip 5 features an integrated microphone and loudspeaker, enabling direct Bluetooth phone calls when your smartphone is within wireless range (roughly 10 meters). When a call comes in, you can accept or decline right from your wrist, conversing directly through the watch speaker if your hands are full cooking dinner or organizing gear in your workspace.

It is vital to understand what this feature is and isn't. This is a convenient tethered extension of your phone's cellular connection, not an independent LTE/cellular smartwatch. You cannot leave your phone behind at home and expect the watch to receive cellular phone calls independently. Furthermore, the watch speaker functions best in quiet indoor spaces; taking a Bluetooth wrist call beside a bustling city intersection or inside a noisy subway car is frustrating for both you and the caller.

For office workers and remote professionals, being able to quickly silence spam calls or take a quick hands-free check-in while pacing around a home office is a surprisingly handy luxury. Just remember to configure your Zepp app notification permissions carefully so your wrist doesn't start buzzing constantly with every group chat alert.

Battery life: read the scenario as carefully as the headline

Manufacturer endurance ratings always sound incredible on product boxes. For the Bip 5, Amazfit advertises up to 10 days of battery life under a "typical usage" scenario, dropping down to around 5 days under heavy utilization. In the real world, your mileage will vary wildly depending on how you configure background health monitoring, screen brightness, app notifications, and GPS workout tracking.

If you enable continuous 24/7 heart-rate monitoring, frequent blood-oxygen checks, sleep breathing quality tracking, and use the built-in GPS for a 45-minute run three times a week, you will likely find yourself reaching for the magnetic charging puck closer to the 4-to-5-day mark. Conversely, if you tone down notification alerts, dim the screen, and only use GPS occasionally, stretching past a full week between charges is entirely achievable.

Compared to mainstream flagship smartwatches that demand nightly recharging, getting multiple days of worry-free operation from the Bip 5 is liberating. Establish a predictable charging habit—such as plugging it in while you shower or sit at your computer—and low-battery anxiety practically vanishes from your routine.

Water use: IP68 is not the same promise as a swimming watch

This is arguably the most critical distinction to keep in mind when evaluating the Bip 5: it carries an IP68 water-resistance rating, not a 5 ATM or swimming-grade pressure certification. According to Amazfit's official operational guidelines, IP68 protects the internal electronics against everyday splashes, sweat, accidental rain, and brief handwashing, but it is not rated for swimming, showering, surfing, or water sports.

If you are an avid lap swimmer, triathlete, or someone who routinely showers with their watch on, the Bip 5 is simply the wrong tool for your lifestyle. Submerging an IP68 wearable in chlorinated pool water or subjecting it to pressurized shower streams risks forcing moisture past the seals. If swimming tracking is a core requirement for your fitness journey, look strictly for watches boasting a 5 ATM or ISO 6425 diving rating.

Health and sleep information: helpful context needs sensible expectations

Driven by the Zepp app ecosystem, the Bip 5 offers continuous optical heart-rate monitoring, blood-oxygen (SpO2) saturation checks, stress tracking, and automated sleep stage analysis (including light, deep, REM, and daytime naps). It also aggregates your daily activity into an intuitive PAI (Personal Activity Intelligence) score designed to encourage consistency.

It is crucial to view these metrics as wellness trends rather than medical-grade diagnostics. Wrist-based optical sensors are susceptible to motion artifacts and fit placement. If your primary goal is tracking general lifestyle improvements—such as noticing your resting heart rate trending downward or ensuring you get consistent overnight sleep hours—the Zepp app provides clean, easy-to-digest charts that motivate positive daily habits.

Remember that wearing a large rectangular watch to bed requires getting used to. While the 26g weight helps immensely, side-sleepers may occasionally feel the case pressing against their wrist. Test overnight wear for a few days to see if it disrupts your sleep quality before relying on its recovery metrics.

Phone setup and notifications can decide whether the watch feels worthwhile

Setting up the Bip 5 requires downloading the Zepp smartphone application (compatible with both iOS and Android), creating an account, and pairing via Bluetooth. The companion app handles watch face customization, firmware updates, and metric synchronization. The software interface is clean, though navigating through deep submenus for advanced sports settings can occasionally feel slightly cluttered.

Notification management is where a budget smartwatch either succeeds or fails. By default, letting every single smartphone app push alerts to your wrist will transform your watch into an incessant, anxiety-inducing buzzing machine. Take fifteen minutes during your initial setup to selectively disable social media spam, promotional emails, and low-priority app pings. Limiting your wrist alerts strictly to calendar reminders, text messages, and essential communication apps transforms the Bip 5 from a distraction into a genuine productivity tool.

What would make us choose it—and what would make us pass?

Choose the Amazfit Bip 5 if: You want a massive, easily readable screen for notifications, appreciate having standalone GPS for casual outdoor runs, need Bluetooth calling capabilities on a budget, and rarely subject your electronics to swimming pools or heavy water sports.

Pass on the Amazfit Bip 5 if: You swim regularly (and need a 5 ATM rating), demand the deep contrast and vibrancy of an AMOLED display, require professional-grade sports training metrics, or prefer traditional compact round watch designs over large rectangular form factors.
