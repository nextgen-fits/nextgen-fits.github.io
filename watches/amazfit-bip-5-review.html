The verdict: a large, simple watch that needs a good reason to be the older option

The Amazfit Bip 5 makes the most sense when its current offer is meaningfully cheaper than the newer alternatives and your needs are straightforward: notifications, ordinary activity tracking, an outdoor workout record and an occasional call with your phone nearby. Its appeal is easy to understand. You get a large rectangular screen without having to buy into a premium smartwatch price tier. The compromise is just as important: this is an older TFT-screen design, so compare the whole daily experience with the Bip 6 before treating a discount as a bargain.

If the two cost almost the same, the decision becomes difficult to make in the Bip 5’s favor. The newer watch’s AMOLED display and different water-use specification are substantial differences, not a fresh strap color. If the price gap is large, however, someone who mainly wants readable alerts and walking records may find the Bip 5 a reasonable starting point. The right question is what the older model leaves out of your own routine. A feature you never use does not become essential simply because it appears on the next box.

The screen: size helps, but TFT and AMOLED are different purchases

Amazfit specifies a 1.91-inch TFT touchscreen for the Bip 5. That gives the watch enough area to display a useful amount of text, which is part of its appeal for notifications. Screen size alone does not tell you how legible a particular watch face will be. Tiny complications, thin lettering and crowded layouts can still make a large display harder to read than a simpler face. Start with the information you need at a glance, then choose a face around it.

The TFT panel is the main visual distinction from the Bip 6’s AMOLED screen. Do not expect the same appearance merely because both watches are rectangular and have large diagonal measurements. Compare them in ordinary light when possible, including the angle at which you normally glance at your wrist. Promotional images tell you which layout the manufacturer wants to show; they do not establish measured outdoor visibility. We have not measured either panel’s brightness or reflection behavior.

For someone moving from an inexpensive fitness band, more room for text can be useful. For someone replacing a recent AMOLED smartwatch, the screen technology is a reason to hesitate. That does not make the Bip 5 unusable. It means the screen should be an explicit compromise in the buying decision, rather than a surprise discovered after delivery. If reading the watch is the thing you care about most, compare this part before counting workout modes.

Case and strap: a light watch can still be a large watch

The published case dimensions are 45.94 by 38.09 by 11.2 millimeters, excluding the sensor base, with a listed weight of 26 grams without the strap. Those are manufacturer figures. The useful interpretation is that low weight and small footprint are different qualities. A light rectangular watch can still spread across a narrow wrist or catch a shirt cuff. Look at its length and width together, rather than translating it into the diameter of a round watch.

Try it with the clothes you actually wear. Bend the wrist back as if typing, pull a jacket sleeve over the watch and check whether the strap holds it in place without needing to be very tight. A loose case that moves around during exercise is a fit issue; overtightening the strap is not an ideal fix. If possible, inspect the sensor position and follow the maker’s wearing guidance. Comfort becomes especially important if sleep tracking is one of your reasons for buying.

The standard strap arrangement also deserves a practical check. Confirm the compatible width and attachment before ordering a replacement, and distinguish the watch package from a bundle supplied by a marketplace seller. A softer third-party strap might suit your taste, but it should not be necessary to make a fundamentally unsuitable case size work. Budget for an accessory only after you know the watch itself fits.

GPS: useful for recording a route, not proof of measured accuracy

The Bip 5 includes onboard satellite positioning. That matters because an exercise route can be recorded by the watch rather than relying entirely on the phone’s positioning. It does not mean the watch replaces every phone function during a walk or run. Calls, notifications, synchronization and any online feature have their own connection requirements. Separate the job of recording an activity from the job of staying connected to people.

We do not have controlled GPS comparisons that establish the Bip 5’s route accuracy against another watch. A satellite-system count cannot do that on its own. Signal conditions, the start procedure, the route and software behavior all affect what ends up on the map. For a practical arrival check, let the watch obtain its required positioning state before starting an outdoor activity, then inspect the recorded route in the app. That is a check for your unit and your routine, not a laboratory ranking.

If your main interest is keeping a casual record of walks and ordinary runs, the presence of onboard positioning can be enough to put the Bip 5 on a shortlist. If pacing accuracy is central to structured training, look for independent tests with named comparison devices and published methods. A review that only repeats the feature list has not answered that question. Nor does a clean promotional map prove how the watch performs among buildings or trees.

Bluetooth calls: keep the phone connection in the picture

The microphone and speaker support Bluetooth phone calling with a compatible connected phone. Think of that as a convenient extension of the phone, rather than a separate cellular watch. It can be useful when the phone is in a bag or on a nearby desk. It is a different proposition from leaving the phone behind and expecting the watch to receive ordinary calls independently. Confirm the connection and permissions during setup rather than learning the limit during an important call.

Call convenience is also different from call quality. We have not recorded microphone comparisons or assessed wind handling, so we cannot rank the Bip 5 against earbuds or a phone on that basis. When you receive it, make a short test call in the environment where you expect to use it and ask the other person whether your voice is clear. If private calls in a shared space are common, a watch speaker may be less useful than keeping headphones available.

Battery life: read the scenario as carefully as the headline

Amazfit publishes up to ten days for typical use and up to five days in its heavier-use scenario. Those are conditional claims, not a schedule promised to every owner. The more useful question is whether the settings behind the claim resemble your own behavior. Frequent alerts, long screen-on time, calls and outdoor activity recording all belong in that comparison. A buyer who wants continuous information should not budget around the lightest possible usage pattern.

Plan a charging routine before you decide that a longer headline number is automatically better. Charging while you shower or sit at a desk may fit your week; charging overnight may interrupt the sleep data you want. Keep the correct magnetic charger accessible and check which cable is included in the actual package. A watch with a longer theoretical interval can still become annoying if its only charger is left in another bag.

For the first week, record your settings and the approximate interval between charges. Change one feature at a time if you need to understand the effect. That gives you a more useful ownership baseline than comparing your result with an unidentified comment from someone using different settings. We do not turn isolated battery reports into an average or a claim that one watch lasts a specific number of days in our own use.

Water use: IP68 is not the same promise as a swimming watch

The Bip 5 carries an IP68 rating, and Amazfit’s model guidance limits the intended water use. Do not borrow the Bip 6’s 5 ATM label or assume that a watch sold for fitness automatically supports pool sessions. Check the current manual for the exact model and planned activity. A general word such as waterproof on a retail listing is less useful than the manufacturer’s actual instructions.

This distinction can settle the purchase immediately. If swimming is a regular part of your week, start with a watch the manufacturer explicitly permits for it and follow the care conditions. If you only need everyday splash tolerance, the calculation may be different. In either case, avoid using a hot shower or an improvised immersion experiment as a test of the watch. A rating also does not guarantee the condition of seals on a damaged or used example.

Health and sleep information: helpful context needs sensible expectations

The Bip 5 includes optical wellness measurements and sleep-related features through the Zepp ecosystem. We have not validated its readings against reference equipment, so the presence of a sensor is not evidence of medical accuracy. Decide which information you want to follow and whether you are comfortable using it as general context. A score should not replace appropriate medical advice or become a diagnosis because it appears on a wrist display.

For ordinary use, consistency is often the useful question: will you wear the device regularly, charge it when needed and review the information in a way that helps your routine? A watch that feels awkward overnight may produce less useful coverage for you than a simpler device you keep wearing. Look at the app as well as the watch. The screen on your wrist is only one part of how records are stored, reviewed and understood.

Phone setup and notifications can decide whether the watch feels worthwhile

Before buying, confirm the current Zepp app requirements against your actual phone. Install the intended app from the normal store, check the permissions required for the functions you want and decide which notifications should reach your wrist. Forwarding every alert can make any smartwatch feel distracting. Start with the people and applications that justify an interruption, then add more only if they prove useful.

Do not assume that every phone supports the same response actions or background behavior. Operating-system permissions, power management and app updates can affect the experience. Check the available function on your own platform, particularly if replies or a specific service are essential. A list of smart features is a starting point for compatibility questions; it is not a promise that the watch duplicates all of your phone’s applications.

What would make us choose it—and what would make us pass?

Choose the Bip 5 when a verified current offer gives you a worthwhile saving, you like the rectangular fit, the TFT screen suits you and the water-use limits fit your routine. It can be a sensible way to find out which smartwatch functions you actually use without paying for a more elaborate device. Confirm the seller, condition, included charger and return terms, especially when older stock or marketplace bundles are involved.

Pass when the price is close to the Bip 6, when swimming is part of the requirement, or when a premium-looking AMOLED display is a priority. Also pass if you need independent cellular calling, a particular app ecosystem or validated training measurements that this evaluation cannot establish. Buy the watch for the functions it demonstrably provides. The best discount is still poor value when it leaves out the reason you wanted a smartwatch in the first place.
