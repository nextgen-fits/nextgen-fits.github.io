What to compare first

Start with the room’s ambient light. A bright window behind you can reflect directly into a glossy display; a dark room with a bright screen creates strong contrast. OSHA recommends considering monitor position relative to windows[cite: 1]. Turning the screen perpendicular to the window can help before adding another lamp.

A desk lamp offers broader placement and can illuminate paper, keyboard or a side table. An articulated arm can aim light precisely, while a shade keeps the bulb from shining directly into your eyes. The tradeoff is a base or clamp that occupies space and can compete with a monitor arm.

Reflections depend on geometry, not the product category. At night, turn the light on and view a dark screen from your normal seat. Move or dim the lamp if you see a bright reflection. Adjust the screen angle and brightness so the display is comfortable rather than overpowering.

Avoid common buying assumptions

Color temperature changes the feel of a workspace, but there is no universal “focus” setting. A tunable light allows a cooler task appearance during some work and a warmer tone in the evening if you prefer. Color rendering may matter for design or craft tasks; check measured specifications.

A low-risk way to decide

Light bars can be poor at lighting paper positioned far from the screen or a large desk corner. Lamps can be poor if their beam creates a hotspot or glare. If both tasks matter, a bar plus low ambient room light may be effective, but avoid adding products without identifying the shadow or reflection you want to fix.

Do not overvalue a single headline number. A specification helps narrow options, but the complete experience also depends on materials, design, compatibility, manufacturing variation and maintenance. Look for the maker’s manual or support page for the exact reference, and compare independent evidence where the difference matters to your decision.

Final pre-purchase checklist

Compare power method, dimming steps, control placement, flicker information, warranty and return window. A simple lamp with a good shade may outperform a premium bar in a particular room. Test after dark and in daylight because the reflection pattern changes with the environment.

Frequently asked questions

Do light bars cause screen glare?

They can if the optics or placement reflect light into your eyes. Check screen compatibility and adjust angle in your room.

Is a desk lamp better for paper?

Often, because it can be aimed at documents; choose a shade or position that controls glare.

What color temperature should I choose?

Personal preference and task matter. Adjustable output helps; avoid assuming one setting suits every time of day.
