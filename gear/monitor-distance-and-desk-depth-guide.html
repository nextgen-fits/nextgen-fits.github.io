Direct answer

There is no single perfect monitor distance for everyone. OSHA gives a general preferred range of about 20–40 inches, while stressing readable text and neutral posture. Start by centering the monitor, sitting back with support, and adjusting text size so you do not lean forward. Desk depth should accommodate that comfortable position plus keyboard space.

Measure the usable desk depth

Measure from your eyes to the screen surface while seated as you actually work, not while leaning toward a ruler. Note the closest and farthest comfortable positions, then check whether the stand base and desk edge leave enough room for your keyboard and forearms.

Start with readable text and relaxed posture

If you cannot read comfortably at a reasonable distance, increase text size or display scaling before moving the screen closer. Small text can make a person lean forward, reducing back support. Distance and legibility should be treated as one adjustment rather than separate shopping specs.

Screen size does not set one perfect distance

Center the primary screen in front of your body. A second display used occasionally can sit at an angle, but frequent head turning may make the setup tiring. Keep documents near the display if reading and typing from paper, and avoid placing the main screen far to one side.

Check the stand and cable clearance

Height matters alongside depth. A screen that is too high can encourage a tilted-back head; one too low can invite slouching. Adjust the chair, monitor stand and screen angle together while keeping feet supported and shoulders relaxed.

Place the keyboard before moving the screen

Room layout may limit depth. A flat panel has a smaller rear profile than older bulky screens, but the stand still projects forward and cables need bend room. A corner desk can provide more diagonal depth; a wall arm can help when safe mounting is possible.

Use scaling before leaning forward

OSHA’s range is guidance, not a medical diagnosis or mandatory measurement. Screen size, eyesight, lens prescriptions, posture and task all matter. Take breaks and follow workplace or clinician guidance where applicable; a new monitor cannot resolve every cause of discomfort.

Recheck the position after an ordinary workday

After moving the display, spend a normal work session checking whether you maintain a supported posture, read without squinting and avoid neck rotation. If symptoms persist or worsen, consult qualified occupational-health guidance instead of buying accessories based on a generic rule.

Frequently asked questions

What is the ideal monitor distance?

Use OSHA’s 20–40 inch range as a general starting point, then adjust for readable text and relaxed posture.

How deep should a desk be?

It depends on the screen, stand and your comfortable reach. Measure the setup instead of choosing a universal desk number.

Should the top of the screen be at eye level?

OSHA suggests the top at or slightly below eye level for many setups; glasses, posture and screen size can change the best fit.
