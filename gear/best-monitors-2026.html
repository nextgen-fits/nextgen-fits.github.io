Five starting points, not five versions of “best”.

Choose a monitor by the job that occupies most of your screen time. A creator connecting a laptop each morning may value charging and a reliable connection more than a high refresh rate. A player may prioritize a supported high-refresh mode. Someone writing on a small desk may benefit more from a manageable screen and useful stand adjustment than from either feature.

This guide narrows those decisions to five candidates. It does not claim to rank every monitor available in 2026, and none of the selections is described as a laboratory-tested winner. If your requirement is specialist color matching, an ultrawide format or competitive play beyond the modes discussed here, treat the list as a starting framework rather than forcing an unsuitable product into your setup.

01

Dell S2725QS

For a detailed everyday work screen

The S2725QS is our starting candidate when the priority is a larger, detailed office canvas with smoother interface motion. Its 4K resolution and up-to-120Hz mode address different needs: one affects available detail, while the other affects update frequency. Use your operating-system scaling to choose readable text rather than assuming that native resolution must mean tiny menus.

The important boundary is connectivity. This model does not provide a USB-C laptop video-and-power connection. If you use a desktop computer or already own an appropriate dock, that may be irrelevant. If your aim is to remove daily laptop connections, compare the ProArt before treating the Dell as the simpler purchase.

Good fit if

You prioritize 4K work and can provide a suitable video connection separately from laptop power.

Look elsewhere if

USB-C charging is essential or your main goal requires independently established high-speed gaming response.

Decision rule: compare it with a USB-C monitor only after including the dock or adapter you would otherwise need. Do not pay twice to solve a connection problem already handled by your existing equipment.

02

ASUS ProArt PA279CRV

For a compatible laptop and a 4K workspace

The PA279CRV combines a 27-inch 4K 60Hz display with USB-C video and power delivery up to 96W. The appeal in this comparison is practical integration: a compatible laptop can receive its display connection and charging through the monitor cable. Confirm the computer's supported video output and power requirements before calling the arrangement one-cable docking.

A connection-oriented choice can be more valuable than a faster screen if plugging in and disconnecting is the part of your workday that needs improvement. Conversely, an existing dock may reduce that advantage. List the peripherals you connect, check what the monitor can accommodate, and identify any remaining adapter before comparing complete costs.

Good fit if

You want a detailed laptop workspace and can use the USB-C connection and available charging power.

Look elsewhere if

You primarily want a higher refresh rate or your laptop requires capabilities this connection cannot provide.

03

AOC Q27G3XMN

For a gaming-oriented 1440p shortlist

AOC specifies a 27-inch 2560 × 1440 VA display with a Mini-LED backlight, 336 local-dimming zones and DisplayHDR 1000 certification. Its manual distinguishes up to 180Hz through DisplayPort from up to 144Hz through HDMI at the documented vertical scan ranges. Use the manual's timing information for the input and source device you intend to connect.

This is the gaming-oriented route in the shortlist. It deserves consideration when higher refresh and local dimming matter more to you than 4K office detail or laptop charging. The specification alone cannot tell us how motion transitions, dark scenes or local-dimming behavior will look to you; we have not performed those measurements.

Good fit if

You want a 1440p gaming candidate and can use the appropriate input and supported mode.

Look elsewhere if

You need a USB-C laptop hub, a 4K workspace or a recommendation backed here by measured esports response testing.

Before choosing: inspect independent measurements of the exact model if motion artifacts or HDR performance will determine your satisfaction. Check whether your graphics hardware can deliver the frame rates you want at your chosen settings. A faster display does not make the computer render faster.

04

Dell P2425H

For a compact office screen with useful adjustment

The P2425H serves a different need from the larger displays. It offers a 23.8-inch Full HD workspace, up to 100Hz and height, tilt, swivel and pivot adjustments. If a large base or badly positioned screen is your current problem, compare the supplied stand with the cost and space requirements of adding an arm to another monitor.

Do not infer a laptop dock from the USB-C connector. On this model the USB-C connection is a downstream peripheral port with up to 15W, not the video-and-charging input offered by a docking monitor. P2425H and P2425HE must not be treated as the same product.

Good fit if

You want a smaller office screen and useful stand movement without planning a separate arm.

Look elsewhere if

You expect one-cable laptop docking or need more resolution for your main application layout.

Choose it for the fit and complete setup, not because every task needs a smaller display. For two readable windows or wide spreadsheets, compare the space you gain from a larger higher-resolution screen before making the final decision.

Do these checks before comparing deals.

Choose resolution for the work, not the label.

Think about what must remain readable at the same time. A reference screen showing one application has a different job from a main display showing a spreadsheet, browser and communication window. Higher resolution can improve detail, but the scale you choose determines how much content remains comfortable to use. Try your normal layout before buying on diagonal size alone.

Write down the full video path.

Include the computer port, adapter or dock, cable and monitor input. Verify the intended display mode at each step. Do not assume that a hub marked 4K supports the refresh rate you want or that a monitor's fastest mode is available on all its inputs. A USB-C-shaped connector is not sufficient evidence of video output.

Compare complete costs.

Account for anything required to use the product as intended, including mounting equipment or a missing receiver on a peripheral bundle. Keep accessories you already own when they work. A display that includes a useful stand or laptop connection may be the more economical arrangement even when its individual price is higher.

Keep untested claims separate from documented features.

A refresh-rate limit does not establish motion clarity. A color-gamut claim does not establish calibration accuracy. A display with HDR support does not automatically provide the same experience as another HDR display. When one of those qualities is central to the purchase, look for exact-model measurements that describe the method and conditions.

Your final buying check

These checks are for your own planning; they are not saved or submitted.

If two candidates remain, return to the feature that would change your working day. Choose the USB-C route when it meaningfully simplifies laptop use, the compact office route when space and adjustment matter, or the gaming route when its supported modes address your needs. If your current display already does the job, keeping it is a valid outcome.

Questions before you buy.

Which monitor is best for everyone?

None of these is a universal winner. They solve different problems, and the recommended route depends on your computer, applications, space and budget.

Is 4K always better than 1440p?

It offers more pixels, but the useful choice depends on scaling, performance needs and the connection your computer can provide. A gaming setup and a document workstation may reasonably prioritize different things.

Does every USB-C monitor charge a laptop?

No. Confirm that the port is an upstream video input with power delivery and that the available power matches the laptop. The P2425H downstream port is not that kind of connection.

Why are there five monitors instead of the old ten?

This revision prioritizes a smaller set with distinct uses and checked destinations. It removes the earlier implication that this article contains measured benchmark results for every product.
