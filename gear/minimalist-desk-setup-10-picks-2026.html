Three plans. You do not need everything.

A minimalist desk is easier to maintain when every purchase has a job. A beautiful photograph cannot tell you whether a mouse fits your hand or whether a laptop will charge through a monitor. Start with the equipment you own, the space available and the task that currently feels awkward. Use that information to choose one of the plans below.

The five products later in this guide are alternatives and optional additions, not a bundle to buy together. There are two display routes, two different mouse shapes and one hub option. You may need none of the accessories if your existing setup already covers those functions. We do not attach a fixed total to a plan because changing offers, variants and accessories can make that total misleading.

Plan 1: keep the screen, clear the surface.

This plan suits a workspace that functions well but feels crowded. Remove items that do not support the next task, move rarely used accessories off the active surface, and route cables along a consistent path. Leave enough slack for devices to move without pulling on ports. Reposition the existing screen before deciding that a larger display or a new arm is necessary.

Start with the keyboard and mouse position you can use naturally, then check where the monitor base, notebook and laptop fit around them. A desk mat is optional: it can define a work area, but it also occupies surface area and needs the right dimensions. Measure the keyboard and mouse movement area before selecting one. A mat that looks ideal in a wide product photograph may be too deep for your table.

Buy only after identifying the remaining problem. A monitor arm may help if the base blocks useful space and the desk supports a suitable mount. A new mouse may help if its shape or controls better suit your work. Neither accessory is necessary simply to make the desk resemble a staged photograph.

First purchase: possibly none.

Spend a normal work session using the rearranged setup. Note which inconvenience persists before adding another item.

Plan 2: reduce the daily laptop connections.

This plan suits someone who arrives with a laptop, plugs in a screen and peripherals, then disconnects everything later. Count the connections you actually make. If the monitor, charger, mouse receiver and storage each need attention, a more integrated setup may help. If you already use a reliable dock, keep that in the comparison rather than treating it as an automatic replacement.

A compatible USB-C monitor can combine display input with laptop charging, while a separate hub can add ports to a screen you already own. Those are different routes to the same practical goal. Check laptop video support, required charging power, peripheral ports and the display mode you need before deciding which route is cheaper and simpler.

For the products below, the ProArt monitor is the integrated-display route. The Anker 555 bundle is a candidate for a compatible laptop connected to an existing screen. Do not automatically buy both. A monitor with useful built-in connections may remove the reason for a hub; conversely, a hub you already own may remove the reason to pay more for monitor connectivity.

Plan 3: build the essentials in two stages.

If you are starting from an empty desk, first secure a display arrangement and input devices that match your computer and work. Keep the keyboard and mouse you already like. A compact screen can be sufficient for one main application, while a larger high-resolution display may be more useful for comparing documents or keeping tools beside a project.

Reserve some budget for any required cable and for positioning the screen. Do not spend the whole allowance on panel specifications and then discover you need an adapter or another support. Compare the complete system cost with the alternative of using a display that includes the connections or adjustments you need.

In the second stage, address problems that remain after normal use. Add a mat if the desk surface needs it, cable management where cables actually collect, and storage for objects that truly stay within reach. A standing desk is a separate furniture decision involving room dimensions, stability, height range and load. It should not be an automatic final item in a small accessory shopping list.

Three checks that prevent an expensive mismatch.

Mounting: match the model, not the brand.

For any monitor arm, compare panel-only weight, mounting pattern, required screws and desk attachment. Check both minimum and maximum loads. Inspect the desk underside for frames or drawers that obstruct a clamp. A glass top, hollow panel or thin edge may need a different mounting approach or may be unsuitable for the chosen mount; follow the desk and mount manufacturers' instructions.

Display: trace the entire connection.

Write the path from computer port to cable or adapter to display input. Confirm the desired resolution and refresh rate at every step. A product having a USB-C socket does not establish video support, and a high-wattage charger does not establish that a hub passes all of that power to the laptop.

Layout: account for movement.

Leave room for a mouse stroke, an open notebook, the monitor base and plugs at the rear. If the desk moves, check the cables across its full travel before securing them. Do not pull power connectors tight to achieve a cleaner photograph. Useful spare length and access to switches matter more than hiding every visible cable.

How to read owner feedback for a whole setup.

For this revision, accessible Amazon review coverage was not sufficient to support a representative owner summary for these candidates. We have therefore removed the earlier unverified star displays and broad claims about what hundreds of owners say. Published specifications support the feature comparisons; individual comfort, durability and reliability remain separate questions.

When reading reviews yourself, identify the exact variant and the problem you need answered. For the mouse, look for hand size, grip preference and connection method. For the hub, look for the laptop model, charger, screen resolution and cable arrangement. For the monitor, look for the named display and how its stand fits the desk. A review without that context may be sincere but still be a poor guide for your purchase.

Separate product faults from shipping damage, a missing bundle item, a computer limitation or a setup mistake. Useful comments explain what was connected and what happened after troubleshooting. A star average cannot tell you that an adapter is compatible with your laptop. Compare critical and positive experiences before deciding which uncertainty remains important.

Your final buying check

These checks are for your own planning; they are not saved or submitted.

Start with the item that removes the most persistent inconvenience. Keep the next purchase optional until you have used the revised setup. This approach may leave you with fewer accessories than the example photographs, which is entirely appropriate when the desk already supports your work.

Questions before you buy.

Do I need to buy all five products?

No. The displays and mice are alternatives; the hub is optional. Keep devices that already perform their job.

Does the linked MX Master 3S include a USB receiver?

The inspected Amazon listing explicitly says No Receiver. Check the current offer and your computer’s Bluetooth support.

Does the Anker bundle mean the hub has a DisplayPort socket?

No. The inspected listing includes a separate USB-C to DisplayPort adapter. Check its intended connection independently of the hub.

Should I buy a monitor arm first?

Only if the existing stand causes a problem and a specific arm fits both the monitor and desk. Otherwise start with positioning and cable routing.
