Three plans. You do not need everything.

A minimalist desk is easier to maintain when every purchase has a specific job. A beautiful lifestyle photograph cannot tell you whether a mouse fits your palm or whether your work laptop will charge through an external monitor. Start with the hardware you already own, the physical dimensions of your workspace, and the task that currently feels awkward or constrained. Use those variables to select one of the practical plans below.

The five products evaluated later in this guide represent distinct functional alternatives rather than a mandatory bundle to purchase simultaneously. There are two display connection pathways, two distinct mouse ergonomics, and one modular hub option. You may require none of these accessories if your existing setup already fulfills those requirements. We intentionally avoid attaching a single fixed price tag to any plan because fluctuating retailer offers, variant options, and accessory bundles render static pricing misleading.

Plan 1: keep the screen, clear the surface.

This approach suits a workspace that functions well mechanically but feels cluttered and visually heavy. Remove objects that do not directly support your immediate daily tasks, relocate secondary accessories away from the primary active surface, and route necessary cables along a clean, consistent path. Ensure adequate slack so devices can articulate without placing tension on physical ports. Reposition your existing monitor before deciding that a larger display or an expensive mounting arm is necessary.

Begin with keyboard and mouse placement that allows neutral shoulder posture, then check where your monitor base, physical notebook, and laptop sit around them. A desk pad is optional: it can visually zone a work area, but it also consumes valuable surface area and requires precise sizing. Measure your available keyboard and mouse movement zone before buying. A pad that looks spacious in a wide-angle product photo may crowd a narrow desk.

Purchase additional gear only after isolating your remaining ergonomic bottleneck. A monitor arm helps if the stock stand base blocks your notebook and your desk structure supports secure clamping. A new mouse helps if its contour or thumb buttons reduce muscle fatigue. Neither accessory is required simply to make your desk resemble an interior design catalog.

First purchase: possibly none.

Spend a normal working day using your rearranged configuration. Note which physical inconvenience persists before introducing another item.

Plan 2: reduce the daily laptop connections.

This plan is tailored for mobile professionals who arrive at their desk with a laptop, plug in multiple cables, and unplug everything at the end of the day. Count the physical connections you make daily: monitor video, wall power, mouse receiver, external storage, and audio. If managing that bundle of cords creates friction, a consolidated setup will streamline your morning routine. If you already utilize a reliable Thunderbolt or USB-C dock, keep that hardware in your evaluation.

A compatible USB-C monitor merges video transmission with high-wattage laptop charging, while a standalone USB hub adds versatile port expansion to an existing screen. These represent two different architectural routes to the same clean result. Verify your laptop's display alt-mode support, required charging wattage, peripheral port count, and target resolution before determining which route suits your budget.

Among our recommendations below, the ASUS ProArt represents the integrated display path, while the Anker 555 hub serves as a mobile accessory for an existing monitor. Avoid purchasing both simultaneously; a monitor with robust built-in power delivery eliminates the need for a separate hub, while an existing hub may save you from upgrading your display.

Plan 3: build the essentials in two stages.

If you are outfitting an entirely empty desk from scratch, prioritize securing a display and input devices compatible with your primary computer. Retain any keyboard and mouse you already enjoy using. A compact 24-inch screen is adequate for single-application focus, whereas a higher-resolution 27-inch canvas supports side-by-side multitasking.

Always reserve a portion of your budget for certified cables and proper screen elevation. Avoid exhausting your entire allowance on raw panel specifications only to discover you lack a video adapter or secure mounting hardware. Compare complete system costs against alternative displays featuring built-in ergonomic adjustments.

During the second stage, address residual workspace friction. Add a desk mat to protect wooden surfaces, cable management trays where wires congregate, and minimalist storage for items that must remain close at hand. A motorized standing desk is a major furniture investment involving room dimensions, mechanical stability, and weight limits; it should never be an afterthought in an accessory shopping list.

Logitech Lift

Mouse route A · a different grip for smaller hands

Logitech designs the Lift specifically for small-to-medium hands, utilizing a 57-degree vertical upright posture. These physical dimensions make it worth evaluating if you want to experiment with a handshake grip angle to reduce wrist pronation. Ergonomic alignment is subjective, and vertical mice are not medical treatments for repetitive strain injuries.

Good fit if

You want to test a vertical grip style and your hand span aligns with the manufacturer's dimensional recommendations.

Look elsewhere if

You prefer traditional flat mice, require a large-hand ergonomic chassis, or cannot easily trial and return unsuited grips.
