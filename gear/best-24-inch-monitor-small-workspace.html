A small desk needs a measured choice

Buying a twenty-four-inch monitor sounds like the simplest decision in the world when you have limited room. Then the package arrives, and reality hits: the factory stand base is far deeper than expected, your laptop screen blocks half your field of view, and the USB-C socket doesn't charge your machine. Screen size is just an initial filter. The real challenge is managing desk depth, viewing distance, and cable clutter.

This guide compares three clearly defined models: the Dell P2425H, Dell P2425HE, and ASUS ProArt PA248QV. These are distinct functional solutions tailored to different working habits, not arbitrary winners in a spec-sheet race. We have analyzed their official technical specifications and broken down the real-world tradeoffs they introduce. We don't make unsubstantiated claims about color gamut scores or sub-millisecond input lag; we focus on how they behave on an actual working desk.

The Dell models feature a 23.8-inch 1920-by-1080 (16:9) display panel. The ASUS features a 24.1-inch 1920-by-1200 (16:10) display panel. We group them into the 24-inch category because that reflects how people actually shop. Before spending your money, identify your main desk headache: Is it a shallow desktop, a tangle of charging cords, awkward neck angles, or lack of vertical document space? Different problems require completely different hardware choices.

Measure width, depth and the objects you need to keep

Clear your workspace and measure the actual usable surface. Don't measure from the edge of the wood if you have a desk lamp base, a headphone stand, or a stack of books permanently occupying the corner. The critical figure is the clear space between where you sit and where the screen must stand.

According to official documentation, the Dell monitors measure approximately 53.9 centimeters wide, with a stand depth of 18.2 centimeters. The ASUS PA248QV measures roughly 53.3 centimeters wide, but its stand requires 21.1 centimeters of depth. That extra three centimeters of base depth might seem trivial on a massive dining table, but on a shallow 50cm or 60cm worktop, it shoves your keyboard uncomfortably close to your wrists.

Mark out these footprints with painter's tape or scrap paper. Place your keyboard and mouse in front of them, position your laptop where you normally open it, and sit down. If your forearms are pushed off the edge of the desk or your nose is eighteen inches from the glass, an oversized stand is going to cause daily frustration regardless of how crisp the image looks.

Choose 16:9 or 16:10 for the work you actually do

The Dell P2425H and P2425HE provide 1080 vertical pixels in the standard 16:9 widescreen format. The ASUS PA248QV delivers 1200 vertical pixels in a taller 16:10 ratio. That 120-pixel difference might sound modest, but in everyday use, it gives you roughly eleven percent more vertical document space.

Think about what you do all day. If you write code, manage deep spreadsheets, or review long PDF contracts, those extra vertical lines mean less endless scrolling and fewer lost rows beneath fixed application toolbars. On the other hand, if your display primarily plays full-screen video or edits widescreen media, a 16:9 panel displays standard content without black letterbox bars.

Both form factors share comparable pixel densities: roughly 93 PPI on the Dell panels and 94 PPI on the ASUS. That means text is rendered at practically identical physical sizes. If your current screen looks blurry, swapping to another 1080p monitor won't magically solve it. First check your operating system scaling, cable standard, and native output settings using our blurry monitor troubleshooting guide before spending money on new hardware.

Dell P2425H: the simpler connection route

The Dell P2425H is designed for people who work with a desktop PC or already own a reliable laptop docking station. Its rear panel offers HDMI 1.4, DisplayPort 1.2, and legacy VGA ports, backed by a fully adjustable ergonomic stand offering 150mm of smooth height adjustment, swivel, tilt, and 90-degree pivot. The panel supports a fluid 100Hz refresh rate over digital inputs.

Here is the critical catch: Dell includes a USB-C port on the side of the chin, but it is strictly a downstream peripheral connection providing up to 15W of power for charging a smartphone or plugging in a flash drive. It does not accept incoming video from your laptop, and it will not power your computer. Do not buy the P2425H assuming it will give you a single-cable laptop setup.

If you connect your computer using standard HDMI or DisplayPort cables and plug your laptop charger directly into the wall, the P2425H is an exceptional, rock-solid workhorse. You get excellent text contrast, 100Hz scrolling smoothness, and top-tier stand ergonomics without paying extra for docking hardware you won't use.

Dell P2425HE: investigate it for a compatible one-cable laptop

The Dell P2425HE takes the same 23.8-inch 100Hz IPS panel and stand from the P2425H, but transforms the rear connectivity into a full-fledged workstation hub. It features an upstream USB-C port with DisplayPort 1.4 Alt Mode, up to 90W of USB Power Delivery, an RJ45 gigabit Ethernet jack, and a DisplayPort-out port for daisy-chaining a second monitor.

For modern laptop users, this completely changes your morning routine. You set your laptop on the desk, connect one USB-C cable, and immediately get video output, high-speed wired internet, full peripheral access, and 90W fast charging. Your bulky laptop charger can stay permanently tucked away inside your backpack.

Before purchasing, check your laptop's hardware specifications. Your computer's USB-C port must support DisplayPort Alt Mode for video output. If you plan to use the DisplayPort-out port to daisy-chain a secondary display via MST (Multi-Stream Transport), note that Windows supports extended dual desktops natively, while macOS will mirror the two external displays unless routed through an active Thunderbolt connection. Consult our Mac dual-display guide to avoid unexpected mirroring issues.

ASUS PA248QV: consider the extra vertical workspace

The ASUS ProArt PA248QV is built for users who prioritize vertical screen real estate and dependable color reproduction. Its 24.1-inch 1920-by-1200 IPS panel delivers a factory-calibrated 100% sRGB color space, a 75Hz refresh rate, and dedicated physical menu buttons for switching color profiles.

Connectivity includes DisplayPort, HDMI, legacy VGA, audio inputs, and a four-port USB 3.0 hub powered by a traditional USB Type-B upstream cable. It does not provide any USB-C laptop video or charging capabilities. If you buy this monitor, plan to use your laptop's standard power brick and an HDMI or DisplayPort connection.

The base of the PA248QV requires 21.1 centimeters of depth—roughly three centimeters deeper than the Dell stands. If your desk is pushed against a wall in a tight room, make sure that extra depth doesn't push the screen directly into your face. For text-heavy workflows, photo editing, and software development, the 16:10 aspect ratio remains one of the most comfortable layouts ever designed.

Stand adjustment first, a monitor arm second

All three displays feature ergonomic factory stands with full height adjustment (150mm on the Dells, 130mm on the ASUS), forward/backward tilt, horizontal swivel, and 90-degree pivot for vertical orientation. They also support universal 100x100mm VESA mounting patterns.

Always try the included factory stand before buying an aftermarket monitor arm. A well-designed stock stand requires zero desk clamping force and sets up in thirty seconds. An articulating arm only becomes necessary if you need to reclaim the flat tabletop space directly beneath the display, or if your desk is so shallow that you need the panel positioned flush against the back wall.

If you decide to install a monitor arm, check both the minimum and maximum weight ratings of the gas spring. Because these 24-inch panels are lightweight (weighing around 3.5kg to 4.2kg without the stand), heavy-duty monitor arms designed for 34-inch ultrawides will fail to stay lowered. Check our monitor arm versus stand comparison for detailed clamp clearance checks.

Refresh rate, connections and peripheral clutter

The Dell P2425H and P2425HE operate at up to 100Hz, while the ASUS PA248QV operates at 75Hz. For everyday office work, stepping up from 60Hz to 75Hz or 100Hz makes mouse cursor tracking, window dragging, and long PDF scrolling noticeably smoother and less fatiguing on the eyes. However, these are productivity-first IPS panels; they are not competitive esports displays with sub-millisecond response overdrive.

Audit your everyday desktop accessories. If you use a wired keyboard, a wireless mouse dongle, a dedicated webcam, and external speakers, plugging them all into the back of your monitor keeps your desk surface completely free of loose cords. When paired with the P2425HE's single USB-C cable, your entire desk functions as a clean, unified dock.

A buying checklist that avoids paying for the wrong upgrade

Compare the three models directly against your physical measurements and current laptop ports:

  • Choose the Dell P2425H if you want an affordable, high-quality 1080p 100Hz office monitor with an outstanding adjustable stand, and you don't need the monitor to charge your laptop.
  • Choose the Dell P2425HE if you want the ultimate clean desk setup with a single USB-C cable handling 90W charging, 100Hz display, and gigabit wired networking.
  • Choose the ASUS PA248QV if your work revolves around coding, spreadsheets, or long-form writing where the 16:10 vertical ratio reduces daily scrolling.

Inspect return policies and seller warranty coverage before checking out. A modest price difference is worth paying if the retailer provides a hassle-free return window in case the physical screen dimensions or stand footprint don't fit your space.

Set it up with one known working connection

When your new display arrives, set it up systematically. Connect your computer using one direct, known-working video cable first. Confirm in Windows Display Settings or macOS System Settings that the panel is running at its native resolution and maximum refresh rate (100Hz on Dell, 75Hz on ASUS).

Only after verifying a clear, flicker-free picture should you connect downstream USB peripherals, webcams, or charging cords. Testing each component individually ensures that if a signal drop occurs, you immediately know whether the issue stems from a faulty cable, a laptop port limitation, or a software setting. If you experience connection hurdles, reference our dedicated USB-C monitor troubleshooting steps.

Questions readers ask

Can the P2425H USB-C socket charge and display my laptop?

No. Dell explicitly identifies that port as a downstream 15W data and charging port for small accessories. It does not accept laptop display signals or charge your computer. If you require single-cable video and power, choose the P2425HE.

Does a wider-looking photograph establish desk fit?

No. Promotional product photos rarely show realistic desk clutter. Always mark out the actual 18cm to 21cm base depth and 54cm width on your tabletop using a tape measure before ordering.
