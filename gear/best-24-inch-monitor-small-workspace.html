A small desk needs a measured choice

A twenty-four-inch monitor sounds like an obvious choice for a small workspace. Then you discover that one stand uses more depth than another, the laptop competes with the keyboard, and the USB-C socket doesn't do what you expected. Screen size is a useful starting filter. It isn't the complete fit or connection check.

This guide compares three real references: Dell P2425H, Dell P2425HE and ASUS ProArt PA248QV. They are examples of different buying routes, not the winners of a laboratory test. We have checked their official specifications and explain the trade-offs those specifications create. We haven't measured their panels, colour accuracy, response times or brightness in a controlled room.

The Dell models use a nominal 23.8-inch, 1920-by-1080 display. The ASUS uses a 24.1-inch, 1920-by-1200 display. We call this the twenty-four-inch class because that describes the practical comparison more honestly than pretending the diagonals are identical. The room photograph is a generated editorial illustration, not a photograph of any of these three models.

Start by describing what fails at your current desk. Is it lack of physical space, too little vertical document area, blurry text, inconvenient cables or a screen that cannot sit where you want it? Different problems need different improvements. Choosing a monitor because it appears in a best-of list can be expensive if the list never identifies your actual problem.

Measure width, depth and the objects you need to keep

Clear the desk enough to measure its usable surface. Record width and depth, then note objects that must stay there: keyboard, mouse area, laptop, lamp and notebook. Don't count the full tabletop if a speaker or storage unit permanently occupies part of it. The useful number is the space left for your working arrangement.

The Dell specifications give a body width of approximately 53.9 centimetres and a stand depth of approximately 18.2 centimetres for these references. ASUS lists approximately 53.3 centimetres of width and 21.1 centimetres of stand depth for the PA248QV. These are published dimensions. They let you sketch a layout, but don't determine where the screen plane will sit in your room.

Mark those footprints with paper or removable tape. Add your keyboard and mouse space, then place the laptop where you actually need it. Sit down and see whether you can use the arrangement without moving something every few minutes. A monitor that physically fits can still leave no useful place to write or reach a port.

A negative result means the monitor body is wider than the space entered. A positive result means only that the arithmetic works; the rest of your layout still needs to be checked.

Choose 16:9 or 16:10 for the work you actually do

The P2425H and P2425HE offer 1080 vertical pixels. The PA248QV offers 1200, making it a 16:10 screen. The extra vertical area may be useful when reading documents, editing text or viewing a spreadsheet. Think about what you would display there rather than treating the ratio as an automatic upgrade for every user.

A practical comparison is a page you use every day. Open a document and a browser sidebar, or a spreadsheet and its toolbar. Ask whether vertical space is the constraint. If you mostly watch widescreen video, the balance may be different. There is no single screen shape that makes every task more efficient for every person.

Resolution and physical size work together. Nominal pixel density is roughly ninety-three pixels per inch for the Dell pair and ninety-four for the ASUS, calculated from their advertised resolutions and diagonals. This is geometry, not a sharpness test. It helps explain why the ASUS offers more vertical pixels without a dramatic jump in density.

If your current complaint is text clarity, don't assume every twenty-four-inch screen will solve it. Check the signal resolution, operating-system scaling and application settings before replacing hardware. Our linked blurry-monitor tutorial walks through those checks. Buying a new panel to fix an incorrect output mode can leave you with the same underlying problem and a smaller bank balance.

Dell P2425H: the simpler connection route

The P2425H is a starting point for someone who wants a compact office-class display and already has a workable video connection. Its official page lists HDMI, DisplayPort and VGA inputs, a USB upstream connection and an adjustable stand. The advertised refresh rate is 100 Hz. Check the modes supported by your chosen connection rather than assuming every input behaves identically.

The particularly important detail is its USB-C downstream port: Dell identifies that port as a data connection with up to 15 W power delivery. It is not the laptop video-and-charging input that many people imagine when they see a USB-C socket. If your main goal is a single cable from laptop to monitor for video and useful charging, don't choose this model on that assumption.

It can still be a sensible option if you already connect video separately and don't mind that arrangement. A desktop computer with an available display output may not benefit much from paying for laptop charging in the monitor. Similarly, a laptop owner who already has a reliable dock should compare the complete existing setup before duplicating functions.

Ask the seller to confirm the exact reference. P2425H and P2425HE are similar-looking names with an important functional difference. Read the port diagram, not just the marketing thumbnail. For a purchase that solves a known problem, one missing letter can matter more than a whole paragraph about slim bezels or a modern workspace.

Dell P2425HE: investigate it for a compatible one-cable laptop

The P2425HE adds a documented USB-C upstream connection with DisplayPort alternate mode and up to 90 W power delivery. Dell also lists Ethernet and a DisplayPort output. These features make it worth investigating when your laptop supports the required video path and can use the supplied charging power. A USB-C plug alone doesn't establish that compatibility.

Check the laptop's exact model and the specific port you intend to use. Some laptops have several similar sockets with different functions. Then check the cable, monitor input and required power. This is a complete path: a compatible monitor can't add video output to a laptop port that doesn't provide it, and a charging-only cable doesn't become a display cable through optimism.

The 90 W figure is a maximum advertised capability under the relevant conditions. It may not match every laptop's full workload requirement. Compare it with your computer manufacturer's guidance. If the battery falls during demanding work, investigate the power path and workload rather than assuming a USB-C monitor makes the original charger unnecessary in every situation.

The DisplayPort output is another detail to verify carefully. Don't assume that a daisy-chain arrangement provides two independent extended desktops on every computer or operating system. Ask about your exact host and planned display modes. For Mac users in particular, the linked dual-display guide helps distinguish a working extended-desktop route from a hub arrangement that simply mirrors the same image.

ASUS PA248QV: consider the extra vertical workspace

The PA248QV offers a different route: a 24.1-inch 16:10 panel, 1920-by-1200 resolution and a listed 75 Hz refresh rate. ASUS lists HDMI, DisplayPort and VGA, with a USB hub. It does not offer the one-cable USB-C laptop video-and-charging route described for the Dell P2425HE. Choose it for its documented arrangement, not for a feature it doesn't have.

The extra document height is its most useful comparison point in this guide. If you spend the day in writing or reference material, use a representative task to decide whether that space matters. A more vertical screen can reduce some scrolling, but the amount depends on your application and scaling. We haven't timed tasks or measured a productivity improvement.

Its stand uses slightly more listed depth than the Dell pair. On a roomy desk, that difference may be unimportant. On a shallow desk with a full-size keyboard and notebook, it could change the plan. Mark the footprint before deciding that similar diagonals mean equivalent physical fit. Check height adjustment against your intended screen position as well.

The ProArt name and manufacturer colour claims are not our own calibration results. If colour-critical work is your reason to buy, investigate the exact workflow, calibration evidence and panel requirements separately. This page helps you choose a small-workspace configuration; it doesn't certify a display for professional colour matching just because the specification sheet mentions creative work.

Stand adjustment first, a monitor arm second

All three models list adjustable stands and a 100-by-100 mm VESA mounting pattern. The Dell pages describe 150 mm of height adjustment; ASUS lists 130 mm. Those figures help you compare available movement, but the starting height and panel geometry also affect where the display ends up. Check the complete dimensional drawing when height is a deciding factor.

Try the supplied stand before buying an arm. It may already offer the position you want with fewer installation questions. An arm becomes useful when you have identified a problem it can solve: freeing a footprint, moving the screen regularly or finding a position the original stand cannot reach. It isn't automatically an improvement on every small desk.

If you investigate an arm, use the monitor's weight without its original stand and include mounted accessories. Check both minimum and maximum supported payload, not only the maximum. The desk, clamp, wall clearance and mounting hardware also need to work together. Our linked arm-versus-stand guide explains that complete check with a useful example of a light panel near an arm's lower limit.

Don't assume mounting makes the whole screen disappear toward the wall. Some arms need rear clearance or fold sideways in a way that affects placement. A compact original stand can outperform an unsuitable arm on a shallow desk. Draw the expected screen plane and cable bend space before purchasing mounting hardware to solve a footprint problem.

Refresh rate, connections and peripheral clutter

Refresh rate is one part of the experience. The listed 100 Hz Dell mode and 75 Hz ASUS mode can guide a connection check, but they aren't our measured motion-performance results. If gaming is a main task, investigate response behavior and the supported gaming features separately. This shortlist is organised around workspace fit and connection needs.

List the peripherals you actually use. A keyboard, mouse receiver, webcam, storage drive and headset can consume ports quickly. Decide whether they need to connect through the monitor or can stay connected to the computer. A hub adds convenience only if the required upstream connection is present and the layout makes its ports usable.

Keep video and USB data requirements separate. It is possible to get a picture while the monitor's USB hub is not connected, or to power a device through a socket that isn't the laptop display input. Use the manufacturer's diagram to trace each function. Labeling the cables during setup can save a lot of later confusion when you disconnect something for travel.

For a mixed home desk, consider where a second computer would connect and how you would switch it. Don't infer a full keyboard-and-mouse switching function from the presence of several video inputs. Ask which features the exact monitor provides and what manual steps remain. A clear ordinary setup can be better than an ambitious arrangement whose behavior is poorly documented.

A buying checklist that avoids paying for the wrong upgrade

Put the three references beside your own measurements. Eliminate any model that cannot fit the usable space or connect through a verified path. Then compare which remaining one solves the original problem most directly. If you need vertical document room, inspect the ASUS route. If you need compatible laptop charging and video through one cable, investigate the P2425HE route.

If basic video and stand adjustment are enough, compare the P2425H's delivered offer with the alternatives. There is no fixed price ranking here because regional stock, seller terms and discounts change. We don't attach an old price to an affiliate search and call it current. Read the destination's selected configuration, included accessories, condition and return terms.

Return conditions are particularly useful for a display you haven't tried. Find out how shipping, faults and dissatisfaction are handled. Check whether the seller describes a manufacturer warranty for your region and which documents are supplied. A modest price difference can be worth considering if the more expensive route makes a mistaken fit or connection choice easier to correct.

Keep a simple record before ordering: reference, dimensions, video path, charging need, usable desk plan and seller terms. Add any missing answer you need from support. You can use the planner below to begin, but the full decision needs this written information. A neat screenshot of a product page is not a substitute for the exact cart configuration.

Set it up with one known working connection

When the display arrives, start with one computer and one direct video connection supported by both devices. Select the correct monitor input and confirm the intended resolution. Add USB data and charging functions separately where appropriate. This makes it easier to identify the cause if something doesn't work instead of blaming several adapters at once.

Use your normal applications for the first checks. Read a document, inspect a spreadsheet and open a call window. Check whether the stand position and desk layout leave room for the things you planned. A monitor can pass a connection test and still be wrong for your workspace; ordinary tasks reveal that quickly.

If USB-C produces no picture, test the host port, cable and designated input using the linked troubleshooting guide. If text is blurry, verify the active resolution and settings before judging the panel. If the monitor will not charge the laptop as expected, inspect its actual upstream power specification and the host's requirement. These are different problems with different checks.

The best twenty-four-inch monitor for a small desk is therefore the one whose body, stand, picture area and connection path fit your particular work. Measure first, choose a documented route, and test it in a simple sequence. You can get a cleaner, more useful workspace without buying the most elaborate monitor or pretending a specification table proves everything about its performance.

Questions readers ask

Can the P2425H USB-C socket charge and display my laptop?

Dell identifies that socket as downstream data with up to 15 W power. It is not the upstream video-and-charging route offered by the P2425HE.

Does a wider-looking photograph establish desk fit?

No. Use the actual body and stand dimensions, then plan the laptop, keyboard, mouse and writing space around them.
